# Seed data sources

Snapshot date for this seed, 2026-07-17. All figures below were retrieved or verified on that date unless a different retrieval date is noted next to the figure. This file is the thesis record of where every reference data number came from, per PLAN.md Phase 1.

Money figures are quoted here in dollars for readability. The CSVs store the same figures as whole cents.

## Port Authority of NY and NJ, crossings.csv and toll_rates.csv, agency PANYNJ

GWB, Lincoln Tunnel, Holland Tunnel, Bayonne Bridge, Goethals Bridge, Outerbridge Crossing.

- Peak and off-peak E-ZPass passenger car rates and windows, effective 4 January 2026. Peak windows are weekdays 6 to 10am and 4 to 8pm, and weekends 11am to 9pm. Peak E-ZPass is $16.79, off-peak E-ZPass is $14.79, Tolls by Mail is $23.30 flat with no time-of-day variation. Source, https://georgewashingtonbridgetoll.com/ (GWB Toll 2026 page, states "Tariff effective 4 January 2026, Source Port Authority of NY and NJ") cross-checked against https://underanewsun.com/nyc-tolls-cars/ which lists identical figures for all six Port Authority crossings, and against the WebSearch summary of https://www.panynj.gov/bridges-tunnels/en/tolls.html and https://nj1015.com/port-authority-toll-hike/ which independently confirm the 4 January 2026 effective date and the 3 percent increase.
- Prior year GWB E-ZPass rate, effective 5 January 2025 through 3 January 2026, peak $16.06, off-peak $14.06. Source, https://georgewashingtonbridgetoll.com/2025-toll-rates (archived 2025 tariff page). Used only for the GWB rows that prove the toll_rate effective-date supersession behavior in the repository test, not seeded for the other five Port Authority crossings since only the GWB history is needed for that test.
- Modeling decision, not a source citation. The reference-data-schema.md design doc's worked example treats only the Hudson River crossings, GWB, Lincoln, Holland, as time-of-day varying, and implies Bayonne, Goethals, and Outerbridge are flat rate. Real Port Authority policy extended peak and off-peak E-ZPass pricing to all six crossings in 2012 and both sources above confirm the same $16.79 peak, $14.79 off-peak, $23.30 Tolls by Mail schedule at Bayonne, Goethals, and Outerbridge as at GWB. This seed follows the verified real schedule rather than the design doc's illustrative simplification, per the Phase 1 instruction that real seeded data is authoritative over the design doc's illustrative numbers. All six crossings are seeded with the same eight-window peak and off-peak schedule.
- Tolled direction. All six Port Authority crossings toll only the New Jersey to New York direction for passenger cars, confirmed by the Port Authority's published E-ZPass savings language describing eastbound tolling at all six crossings. Seeded as FORWARD with the eastbound or into-New-York compass bearing.

## MTA Bridges and Tunnels, crossings.csv and toll_rates.csv, agency MTABT

Verrazzano-Narrows, RFK Triborough, Queens Midtown Tunnel, Hugh L Carey Tunnel, Bronx-Whitestone, Throgs Neck, Henry Hudson.

- E-ZPass and Tolls by Mail passenger car rates effective 4 January 2026. Verrazzano-Narrows, RFK, Queens Midtown, Hugh Carey, Whitestone, and Throgs Neck are each $7.46 E-ZPass and $12.03 Tolls by Mail. Henry Hudson is $3.42 E-ZPass and $8.87 Tolls by Mail. Source, WebSearch summary citing https://www.mta.info/fares-tolls/tolls/vehicle-types and https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/news/2026/01/02/mta-fare-and-toll-hikes-to-take-effect-sunday, describing a 7.5 percent increase applied 4 January 2026, cross-checked against the table at https://underanewsun.com/nyc-tolls-cars/.
- None of these seven crossings vary by time of day for passenger cars, seeded as a single flat row per crossing per payment type, mask 1111111, window 00:00 to 24:00.
- Tolled direction. Every MTA Bridges and Tunnels crossing modeled here tolls both directions. Verrazzano-Narrows returned to two-way tolling on 1 December 2020 after 34 years of one-way tolling, source https://www.mta.info/press-release/verrazzano-narrows-bridge-split-tolling-to-take-effect-at-midnight-dec-1 as summarized by WebSearch. The other six crossings toll both directions under current MTA policy. Seeded as BOTH.

## MTA Congestion Relief Zone, congestion.csv

- Passenger vehicle E-ZPass peak $9.00, E-ZPass overnight $2.25, Tolls by Mail peak $13.50, Tolls by Mail overnight $3.30. Peak is weekdays 5am to 9pm and weekends 9am to 9pm, overnight is all other times. Program effective 5 January 2025 and unchanged as of the snapshot date. Source, WebSearch summary citing https://www.mta.info/fares-tolls/tolls/congestion-relief-zone/about and https://www.mta.info/fares-tolls/tolls/congestion-relief-zone/faq (both returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch, figures taken from the search engine's indexed summary of the same MTA pages), cross-checked against the $9.00 peak and $2.25 overnight figures independently confirmed in the MTA Bridges and Tunnels rate search above.
- Once per day charge, true, confirmed by the same sources describing the charge as levied once per calendar day regardless of the number of zone entries.
- Crossing credit, up to $3.00 for passenger vehicles at each of the four zone-entry tunnels, Lincoln, Holland, Queens Midtown, and Hugh L Carey, during peak only, for E-ZPass payers. Source, WebSearch summary citing the MTA Congestion Relief Zone E-ZPass page. No credit is published for Tolls by Mail payers, so credit rows are seeded for EZPASS only.
- Modeling decision, not a source citation. The reference-data-schema.md design doc's illustrative example describes a larger credit at the two Hudson River tunnels than at Hugh Carey and Queens Midtown. The verified published credit is a flat $3.00 at all four tunnels for passenger vehicles, with no published difference by tunnel. This seed uses the verified flat figure at all four tunnels rather than the design doc's illustrative tiering.

## NJ Turnpike Authority, crossings.csv and toll_rates.csv, agency NJTA, codes NJTPK and GSP

- New Jersey Turnpike, full length Exit 1 to Exit 18E, Class 1 passenger car, approximately $21.88 with NJ E-ZPass and approximately $22.00 cash, cited as the 2026 schedule. This full-length trip is used as the representative NYC-relevant NJTPK segment, since the reference-data-schema.md design doc directs Phase 1 to model NJTA roadway segments as a single representative point and toll for the covered corridor rather than a true entry-exit matrix, and a full-length trip is a realistic proxy for a weekend rental leaving the NYC area southbound. Source, WebSearch synthesis of the njta.gov toll calculator and toll schedule pages, https://www.njta.gov/travel-resources/toll-calculator/toll-schedules-new-jersey-turnpike/, which could not be fetched directly for the underlying PDF figures, so this is the most defensible published figure found within the research window rather than a directly quoted primary source. Marked as an estimate.
- Garden State Parkway, mainline barrier plaza, approximately $2.30 with NJ E-ZPass, post an 8 cent 2026 increase. Source, https://tollguru.com/new-jersey-toll-road-ez-pass-bridge-tunnel-turnpike, fetched directly. The cash figure of $2.55 is not independently confirmed and is estimated from the generally cited 6 to 15 percent E-ZPass discount versus cash on NJTA facilities, midpoint 10 percent, applied to the E-ZPass figure. Marked as an estimate.
- Both NJTPK and GSP are modeled as flat, no time-of-day variation, matching the reference-data-schema.md statement that NJTA facilities charge a flat amount with no time-of-day variation. Tolled in both directions, BOTH, since NJTA's ticket and barrier system charges regardless of direction of travel.
- Effective date seeded as 2026-01-01, matching the general NJTA 2026 fiscal year toll adjustment cited across the Turnpike and Parkway toll increase coverage, for example https://newjerseymonitor.com/briefs/parkway-turnpike-tolls/.

## NY State Thruway Authority, crossings.csv and toll_rates.csv, agency NYSTA, codes CUOMOBRIDGE and THRUWAY

- Governor Mario M Cuomo Bridge, formerly Tappan Zee, passenger car, $7.25 NY E-ZPass, $12.69 Tolls by Mail, flat with no time-of-day variation, effective 1 January 2026. Tolled only in the eastbound, Westchester-bound, direction. Source, WebSearch summary citing thruway.ny.gov toll schedules and confirmed by a second independent source describing the prior year rate of $6.75 stepping up to $7.25 effective 1 January 2026 under the bridge's published annual 50 cent increase schedule. Direction confirmed by WebSearch summary of mariomcuomobridge.ny.gov FAQ content, tolls collected only southbound and westbound is incorrect, the correct direction per the FAQ summary is tolled only toward Westchester, seeded as FORWARD with an eastbound bearing.
- New York Thruway mainline, representative NYC-relevant segment. The Thruway publishes a per-mile rate rather than a point toll, 4.7 cents per mile NY E-ZPass and 5.8 cents per mile Tolls by Mail for passenger cars in 2026. Source, WebSearch summary citing thruway.ny.gov toll adjustment documentation. This seed computes a representative segment toll by applying the published per-mile rate to a 90 mile representative distance, approximating the Yonkers to mid-Hudson Valley stretch relevant to the Manhattan to Hudson Valley weekend trip named in PLAN.md Phase 2's golden routes, giving $4.23 E-ZPass and $5.22 Tolls by Mail. This is a computed figure built transparently from an officially published per-mile rate, not an independently published point toll, and is marked as such rather than presented as a directly quoted single number.
- The task description directed checking data.ny.gov for NY Thruway data. data.ny.gov's toll dataset was not reachable through the available search and fetch tools within the research window, so the thruway.ny.gov per-mile rate and the Cuomo Bridge figures above were used instead as the defensible published source.

## Rental company toll programs, toll_programs.csv

All company program figures were retrieved from each company's own current policy page and cross-checked against at least one independent secondary summary where the primary page returned only a heading with no body text to the fetch tool.

- Hertz PlatePass, pay-per-use, $9.99 administrative fee per calendar day a toll is incurred, USAGE_DAYS, tolls billed separately at the toll authority's highest undiscounted rate, MAX_CASH_RATE. No specific per-rental cap dollar figure is published, PlatePass states only that per-use fees are capped at an unpublished weekly maximum, so cap_cents is seeded null rather than a fabricated number. Source, https://legalclarity.org/how-hertz-toll-charges-work-fees-rates-and-disputes/, a secondary summary, used because https://www.hertz.com/us/en/products-and-services/value-added-services/united-states/plate-pass returned only its page heading to the fetch tool.
- Hertz PlatePass All-Inclusive, Northeast E-ZPass region, $27.99 per day flat, RENTAL_DAYS, tolls included. A published weekly price point of $139.95 exists, which is exactly five days of the daily rate, but this is a weekly package price rather than a per-rental fee cap in the sense the schema models, so cap_cents is seeded null consistent with the design doc's default for the unlimited program shape. Source, https://cartipsdaily.com/hertz-platepass-cost.
- Avis e-Toll, standard pay-per-use, $6.95 per day, charged for every remaining day of the rental once a toll is first incurred, not only on usage days, confirmed by the exact language "once you pass through an electronic toll, you will pay a convenience fee for each day of the entire rental period, including any days on which e-Toll is not used, up to a maximum cap." ALL_RENTAL_DAYS, capped at $34.95 per rental of up to 30 days, tolls billed separately at the toll authority's highest undiscounted rate, MAX_CASH_RATE. Source, https://www.avis.com/en/products-and-services/services/avis-e-toll, fetched directly.
- Avis e-Toll Unlimited, $10.99 to $25.99 per day depending on checkout location, charged every rental day regardless of usage, RENTAL_DAYS, capped at $54.95 to $181.93 per week, tolls included. The seed uses $25.99, the high end of the range, since the checkout location for this project is the New York metro where the published rate sits at the top of the range, and the schema does not model per-location price variation in Phase 1. Source, https://www.avis.com/en/products-and-services/services/avis-e-toll.
- Budget e-Toll and e-Toll Unlimited, same shapes and figures as Avis, both are Avis Budget Group brands using the same program design. Budget's own FAQ states a $34.75 cap rather than Avis's $34.95, a small but real difference between the two brands' published terms, both seeded as verified. Source, https://budget.com/en/products-services/services/e-toll and https://budget.e-tolls.com/Faq.
- Dollar and Thrifty PlatePass, pay-per-use, $9.99 per calendar day a toll is incurred, USAGE_DAYS, tolls billed separately at the highest undiscounted rate, MAX_CASH_RATE, same PlatePass vendor terms as Hertz. Source, WebSearch summary citing platepass.com Dollar and Thrifty program pages.
- Dollar and Thrifty PlatePass All-Inclusive, $21.49 per day flat, RENTAL_DAYS, tolls included. Source, WebSearch summary citing thrifty.com and dollar.com PlatePass value-added-service pages.
- Enterprise TollPass, $4.95 to $5.35 per usage day, USAGE_DAYS, capped at $34.65 per rental, plus the toll amount. The seed uses $4.95, the published low end. Unlike Hertz, Avis, Budget, Dollar, and Thrifty, Enterprise's published language does not state a marked-up undiscounted billing rate for the pass-through toll, so this program is seeded as EZPASS_RATE rather than MAX_CASH_RATE. This is the weaker-evidence assumption of the program set and is flagged here rather than silently modeled the same as the other companies. Source, WebSearch summary citing enterprise.tollpassgo.com and the Enterprise TollPass guide.
- Congestion Relief Zone coverage. Every program checked, Hertz PlatePass All-Inclusive, Avis and Budget e-Toll Unlimited, and Dollar and Thrifty PlatePass All-Inclusive, explicitly excludes NYC Congestion Relief Zone charges from its flat-fee coverage, confirmed by matching language on each company's own page or FAQ. This contradicts the reference-data-schema.md design doc's illustrative assumption that unlimited plans typically cover congestion. Every program in this seed is seeded with covers_congestion false, reflecting the verified real terms rather than the design doc's illustrative default. Enterprise TollPass explicitly does pass through the Congestion Relief Zone charge as a billed toll under its usual per-toll billing, which is also covers_congestion false since the charge is not waived, only billed through the program like any other toll.
- Effective date. None of the six companies publish a dated effective-date for their toll program terms the way the toll agencies publish tariff effective dates. Every toll_program row is seeded with effective_date 2026-07-17, the date these live policy pages were retrieved and verified for this seed, which is the honest reading of the schema's effective_date column for a company policy that has no published effective date of its own.

## Vehicle class default MPG, vehicle_classes.csv

The eight default_mpg values are approximate, typical EPA combined fuel economy figures by rental car size class, used only as the Phase 4 fallback when a specific make and model cannot be looked up against the EPA fueleconomy.gov web service. These are not tied to a single citable source and are not load-bearing for any Phase 1 acceptance criterion, they exist only so the vehicle_class table has a non-null value in every row. The MIDSIZE figure, 30.5, was already fixed by reference-data-schema.md's own worked example and is kept unchanged so the design doc's example row stays literally correct against this seed.

## Rental pricing calibration, rental_calibration.csv

The base daily rate, company multiplier, and flat fee figures in this file are provisional
placeholder figures, not researched real rates. They exist only so SyntheticRentalProvider has a
deterministic per company and per car class pricing model for development, unit tests, and load
testing, where the acceptance criterion is that identical inputs always produce identical output,
not that the dollar figures match any real company's current pricing. Real calibration numbers
require manually collecting 30 to 50 quotes from Kayak, Costco Travel, and company sites across
companies, car classes, dates, and lead times, which PLAN.md's Phase 9 open item for the user
still lists as outstanding manual work. Replace this file's figures once that collection happens,
the shape of the CSV, one row per company and car class, is meant to still fit the real numbers
without a schema change.
